# BI Analyst Onboarding & Preparation Notes  
**Project:** Enterprise BI Reporting Lifecycle – Case Study  
**Role:** BI / Reporting Business Analyst  
**Author:** Agnes Offenbek  
**Date:** Week 1 (Day 1–5)

---

## 1. Purpose of This Document

This document captures the **initial onboarding phase** and **preparatory activities** performed as a newly hired BI / Reporting Business Analyst.

Its purpose is to:
- document the analyst’s **initial understanding of the BI landscape**
- clarify **context before solution design**
- outline the **thinking and approach** taken before producing formal BI deliverables
- provide transparency into **how the first official project document was defined**

This document is intentionally **non-technical** and precedes any requirements, data modeling, or reporting design.

---

## 2. Role Context and Initial Assignment

### 2.1 Role Context

I joined the organization as a BI / Reporting Business Analyst responsible for:
- supporting business-driven reporting and BI initiatives
- acting as an interface between business, IT, and data teams
- structuring ambiguous reporting needs into clear, testable deliverables
- ensuring alignment with data governance and reporting standards

### 2.2 Initial Assignment (High-Level)

During initial discussions with my manager and stakeholders, the following high-level need was identified:

> “Our current management reporting around insurance performance is fragmented, inconsistent, and difficult to interpret across systems.”

At this stage:
- no clear scope was defined
- requirements were not yet formalized
- multiple stakeholders had overlapping but different expectations

The immediate goal was **not to build a report**, but to **structure the problem space**.

---

## 3. Onboarding & Context-Building Activities (Day 1–3)

### 3.1 Stakeholder Introductions

Initial meetings were held with representatives from:
- Business / Management
- Finance / Controlling
- Claims and Policy Operations
- IT / Data / BI team

The objective of these meetings was to:
- understand decision-making responsibilities
- identify pain points with existing reports
- clarify expectations toward the BI function

---

### 3.2 Review of Existing Reporting Landscape

I reviewed:
- existing management and operational reports
- Power BI dashboards currently in use
- ad-hoc Excel-based reports
- available documentation (where present)

Initial observations:
- overlapping KPIs with different definitions
- inconsistent data refresh logic
- unclear ownership of reports
- limited documentation explaining “why” reports exist

---

### 3.3 Initial Business Questions Identified

From early discussions, recurring business questions emerged, such as:
- How is overall insurance performance evolving over time?
- Which products or segments drive claims and losses?
- How reliable and comparable are KPIs across departments?

These questions were noted **without attempting to define KPIs yet**.

---

## 4. Key Observations & Risks Identified

### 4.1 Observations

- Reporting issues are not purely technical; they are **structural and governance-related**
- Business stakeholders often focus on outputs, not definitions
- IT teams lack clear, stable business specifications

### 4.2 Initial Risks

- Misaligned expectations between stakeholders
- Scope creep if requirements are not clearly defined early
- Rebuilding reports without addressing root causes

These risks reinforced the need for a **structured planning phase**.

---

## 5. Decision: Establish a Formal Planning Baseline

Based on the above, I concluded that the project must start with:
- a shared understanding of business context
- clearly identified stakeholders
- explicitly stated objectives and constraints

Before any technical design or development, a **baseline planning document** was required.

---

## 6. Definition of the First Official Deliverable

### Selected First Deliverable:
**Business Context & Stakeholder Overview**

### Rationale:
- aligns all parties on *why* the initiative exists
- provides a stable reference point for future requirements
- reduces ambiguity before entering analysis and design phases
- supports structured communication between business and IT

This document will serve as the **foundation** for:
- Business Requirements Document (BRD)
- BI architecture and data model design
- Test planning
- Reporting and governance documentation
